Case Study

Signs of the Palace Recognition Using Machine Learning Algorithms

"Signs of the Palace" is an easy-to-use web app that helps people communicate palace-related ideas using Indian Sign Language (ISL). It's especially helpful for those who are hearing or speech impaired, making it easier for them to share and learn about historical palaces through sign language.

1. Introduction & Motivation

Sign language recognition (SLR) is an important research area that helps bridge communication gaps. Most initial introductions of sign language detection are largely oriented toward popular sign languages like ASL or general ISL. As a result, a great number of culturally coded and region-specific gestures are overlooked.

This project focuses directly on Palace Sign Language (PSL)—a system of signs denoting royal artifacts, rituals, and symbols linked with Mysore Palace (e.g., Ambari, the Crown, the Sword, the Elephant). By blending AI, video technology, and a love for culture, this project opens up new ways for everyone to explore and celebrate history, making it accessible and engaging.

2. The Dataset

The dataset used in the "Signs of the Palace" project is a custom-created video dataset specifically focused on 29 palace-related objects and symbols.

  • 29 Classes: Includes "Mysore Palace", "King's Crown", "Sword", "Elephant", "Queen", "Main Gate", "Chamundeshwari", "Cannon", and many others.
  • 75 Videos per Class: Each clip is around 3 to 4 seconds long, recorded to maintain consistency in gesture style and clarity.
  • Processing: The videos are processed at 2 frames per second (FPS), meaning exactly 8 frames are extracted from each clip to feed into the deep learning model.

Data augmentation (flipping, rotating, brightness adjusting) was applied to make the training material more robust.

3. Methodology & Architecture

The system utilizes a CNN + LSTM hybrid deep learning architecture. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are capitalized on to derive important spatial features (shapes, edges) out of video inputs, while Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks capture the temporal motion patterns across frames.

5. Results and UI

The system was trained using 80% of the dataset (1950 videos) and tested on the remaining 10% (225 videos). The model achieved a high accuracy of over 93%, showing strong generalization.

The frontend interface allows users to upload a video showing palace-related gestures. Once uploaded, the video is sent to the Flask server for prediction. The result is displayed dynamically on the page, showing:

  • The predicted class name (e.g., "Ambari").
  • An authentic image of the historical object.
  • A styled paragraph box containing the detailed cultural and historical description from `Description.py`.

This makes the result both visually informative and user-friendly, blending heritage education with smooth web functionality.

6. Conclusion & Future Enhancements

The project successfully demonstrates how deep learning can be integrated with cultural education to interpret sign gestures representing historical elements of the Mysore Palace. It showcases the power of AI in recognizing temporal gestures while preserving and promoting regional heritage in an interactive way.

Future Scope:

  • Real-Time Gesture Recognition: Integrate webcam-based live gesture recognition to eliminate the need for uploading pre-recorded videos.
  • Mobile App Deployment: Develop a cross-platform mobile application (using Flutter or React Native) for easier and more portable user access.
  • Multilingual Support: Display gesture class descriptions in multiple languages, including Kannada, Hindi, and English.
  • Expanded Dataset: Include more historical objects, figures, and places related to South Indian heritage.
